Privacy Policy

Effective 18 August 2026 · Last updated 18 October 2026 · Deutsch

Mealog is built so that your meal log stays with you. This policy explains exactly what the app does with your photos, your voice, your profile and your entries — and what it never does with them.

2. What Mealog processes

Only what is needed to turn a meal into an entry, plus whatever you choose to add:

  • Photos you take of your food.
  • Voice input when you describe a meal out loud. The recording is used only to produce a transcript and is deleted as soon as it has been transcribed; only the text is kept.
  • Text you type — dish names, portions, corrections and notes.
  • Your meal log — entries, times, estimated calories and macros, streaks and the photos attached to each entry.
  • Your profile, if you fill it in — your goals (daily calorie and macro targets, optional fibre, sugar and sodium limits, a dietary pattern, and any ingredients you ask Mealog to flag) and, optionally, body details (age, height, weight, sex, activity level). All of this is optional; you can leave it blank, change it, or delete it at any time.

Mealog has no sign-up, asks for no name, address or date of birth, and does not read your contacts or location.

Apple Health is optional and off until you connect it. If you turn it on in Settings, Mealog reads your height and weight to pre-fill your goals and writes the nutrition of meals you log back to Health. Health data stays on your device, is never sent to OpenAI, our backend, or anyone else, and you can revoke access at any time in Settings or in the Health app.

3. Where your log lives

Your meal log is stored on your iPhone. It is not uploaded to a Mealog account, because there is no Mealog account. Deleting an entry removes it; deleting the app removes the whole log. If you back your phone up to iCloud, the log is included in that backup under Apple's terms, not ours.

Optional iCloud sync. If you turn on iCloud sync in Settings — it is off by default — your log (entries, photos and profile) is also stored in your own private iCloud database so it can appear on your other devices. That database is Apple's, governed by Apple's terms; it is tied to your Apple Account and we cannot access it. Turning sync off, or deleting an entry on a synced device, removes it from iCloud too.

4. How AI estimation works

AI estimation runs only after you have agreed to it — Mealog asks the first time, and you can withdraw that agreement, or log meals by hand without any AI, at any time in Settings.

When you log a meal with AI, the photo, voice transcript or text is sent over an encrypted connection to OpenAI, which analyses it and returns the estimate you see. If you have set goals, your daily calorie target, your protein, carbohydrate and fat targets, your dietary pattern and any ingredients you asked to flag are included with the request, because they help the estimate. Your body details — age, height, weight, sex and activity level — are not sent; they stay on your device. If you use the in-app Coach, a summary of your recent log is sent to OpenAI so it can answer you, together with whatever your question requires it to look up.

The request passes through a small backend of ours hosted on Vercel, which forwards it to OpenAI and nothing more — it does not store the photo, voice transcript, text or the estimate.

OpenAI states that content submitted through its API is not used to train its models, and may retain the inputs for a short period (currently up to 30 days) to monitor for misuse before deleting them. OpenAI processes the request in the United States, so logging a meal with AI involves a transfer of that content abroad.

Meal logs say something about your health. What you eat, when you eat and how much can reveal a great deal, which is why Mealog keeps the log on your device, shows no ads and runs no analytics. Treat your device passcode as the lock on that data.

5. What Mealog never does

  • No analytics, telemetry or crash-reporting SDKs.
  • No advertising, ad identifiers or tracking across apps and sites.
  • No selling, renting or trading of your data — to anyone, ever.
  • No profiles built about you, and no automated decisions with legal effects.

6. Who else is involved

  • OpenAI — analyses photos, voice transcripts and text to produce nutrition estimates (processing in the United States).
  • Vercel — hosts the pass-through backend that forwards those requests.
  • Apple — distributes the app and handles any purchase; and, if you turn on iCloud sync, stores your private iCloud database. Apple's own privacy policy governs the App Store, your purchases and iCloud.

That is the complete list. No one else receives your data.

7. How long anything is kept

On our side we keep no photos, voice transcripts, text or estimates. OpenAI may hold the inputs for up to 30 days as described above. On your side, entries stay until you delete them or remove the app — and, if iCloud sync is on, until you delete them from your synced devices.

8. Your rights

Under the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection — and under the GDPR if you are in the EU or EEA — you may request access to your personal data, correction, deletion, restriction, objection or portability, and you may complain to a supervisory authority (in Switzerland, the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner).

In practice most of these you can exercise yourself in seconds, because the data is on your phone: edit an entry to correct it, delete an entry or the app to erase it, export your log to take it elsewhere, and clear or disable your profile and Apple Health access in Settings. For anything else, email v.skromny@bluewin.ch and you will get an answer within 30 days.

9. Security

Everything leaving the app travels over encrypted connections (TLS). Your log is protected by your device's own security — a passcode or Face ID is the single most effective thing you can do to protect it. No system is perfect, and we do not claim otherwise.

10. Children

Mealog is not directed at children under 16 and should not be used by them without a parent or guardian's involvement.

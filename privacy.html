Mealog is built so that your meal log stays with you. This policy explains exactly what the app does with your photos, your voice, your profile and your entries — and what it never does with them.

2. What Mealog processes

Only what is needed to turn a meal into an entry, plus whatever you choose to add:

  • Photos you take of your food.
  • Voice input when you describe a meal out loud. The recording is sent to OpenAI only to produce a transcript, and is deleted from your device as soon as it has been transcribed; only the text is kept.
  • Text you type — dish names, portions, corrections and notes.
  • Your meal log — entries, times, estimated calories and macros, streaks and the photos attached to each entry.
  • Your profile, if you fill it in — your goals (daily calorie and macro targets, optional fibre, sugar and sodium limits, a dietary pattern, and any ingredients you ask Mealog to flag) and, optionally, body details (age, height, weight, sex, activity level). All of this is optional; you can leave it blank, change it, or delete it at any time.
  • Product events, a short list of moments in the app (first launch, finishing onboarding, seeing the subscription screen, a purchase), with a random app-generated ID. They never contain meal content or health values. Details, and how to turn them off, are in section 5.

Mealog has no sign-up, asks for no name, address or date of birth, and does not read your contacts or location.

Apple Health is optional and off until you connect it. If you turn it on in Settings, Mealog reads your height and weight to pre-fill your goals and writes the nutrition of meals you log back to Health. The nutrition Mealog writes into Health stays on your device and is never sent to OpenAI, our backend, or anyone else. A height or weight you read in becomes an ordinary profile value, handled like the rest of your profile (see the AI section below). You can revoke access at any time in Settings or in the Health app.

3. Where your log lives

Your meal log is stored on your iPhone. It is not uploaded to a Mealog account, because there is no Mealog account. Deleting an entry removes it; deleting the app removes the whole log. If you back your phone up to iCloud, the log is included in that backup under Apple's terms, not ours.

Optional iCloud sync. If you turn on iCloud sync in Settings — it is off by default — your log (entries, photos and profile) is also stored in your own private iCloud database so it can appear on your other devices. That database is Apple's, governed by Apple's terms; it is tied to your Apple Account and we cannot access it. Turning sync off, or deleting an entry on a synced device, removes it from iCloud too.

4. How AI estimation works

AI estimation runs only after you have agreed to it — Mealog asks the first time, and you can withdraw that agreement, or log meals by hand without any AI, at any time in Settings.

Meals, goals and your log can reveal information about your health, so Mealog treats what it sends for AI estimation as sensitive data. The legal basis for sending it to OpenAI is your explicit consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a) and 9(2)(a); Swiss FADP Art. 6(7)), given on the AI-consent screen before the first AI request. You can withdraw it at any time in Settings > AI & data; withdrawing stops AI estimation, and manual logging keeps working without it.

When you log a meal with AI, the photo, the voice recording (for transcription), the transcript or the text is sent over an encrypted connection to OpenAI, which analyses it and returns the estimate you see. If you have set goals, your daily calorie target, your protein, carbohydrate and fat targets, your dietary pattern and any ingredients you asked to flag are included with the request, because they help the estimate. Your body details — age, height, weight, sex and activity level — are not sent when a meal is estimated; they stay on your device. The one exception is the in-app Coach: when you chat with it, it sends a summary of your recent log and — so it can answer questions about your goals — may read and send those profile body details, including a height or weight you imported from Apple Health. The in-app consent screen states this before you turn the Coach on.

The request passes through a small backend of ours — hosted on Cloudflare, with Vercel kept as a fallback used only if Cloudflare is unreachable — which forwards it to OpenAI. As our hosting provider, it processes the technical connection data needed to do that, such as your IP address and the time of the request; it does not store the photo, the voice recording, the transcript, the text or the estimate.

To keep the service available to everyone, our backend limits AI requests per day. It counts them against your IP address: the backend (Cloudflare) uses the IP address only as the key of a counter (the number of AI requests that day, UTC) and deletes that counter within 2 days after your last AI request. The app sends no device or installation identifier with AI requests, and the IP address is not stored with your photos, voice, text, profile or estimates. (The separate analytics ID in section 5 never travels with an AI request.) A separate monthly usage total is kept without any IP address. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in keeping the service available and its costs under control (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f); Swiss FADP Art. 31(1)). You can object by email or simply turn off AI estimation. The IP address is never used to track you across apps or websites.

OpenAI states that content submitted through its API is not used to train its models, and may retain the inputs for a short period (currently up to 30 days) to monitor for misuse before deleting them. OpenAI processes the request in the United States, which does not by default provide data protection equivalent to Switzerland's or the EU's. This transfer rests on your explicit consent, given in the app before the first AI request (Swiss FADP Art. 17(1)(a); GDPR Art. 49(1)(a)).

Meal logs say something about your health. What you eat, when you eat and how much can reveal a great deal, which is why Mealog keeps the log on your device and shows no ads. The only usage information Mealog sends is a few product events, with a random ID, listed in section 5; they carry no name, account, meal content or health values. There is no advertising and no tracking across apps. Treat your device passcode as the lock on that data.

5. Product analytics

To understand where people get stuck between installing Mealog, finishing onboarding, seeing the subscription screen and subscribing, and to fix those steps, the app can send a small number of product events. The controller is the same as in section 1: V. Skromny.

What is sent: these events, and nothing else.

  • first_open — the first launch after installing. Extra details: none.
  • onboarding_completed — you finish the intro. Extra details: none.
  • paywall_shown — the subscription screen appears. Extra details: trigger, where it was opened from (onboarding or profile).
  • complete_purchase — a subscription purchase is confirmed by Apple. Extra details: product_id (monthly or annual plan), completed, offer_type (free trial, intro, promo or none), intro_offer (yes/no).
  • purchase_pending — a purchase is waiting for approval, e.g. Ask to Buy. Extra details: product_id, completed.

Each event also carries the time it happened and a random analytics ID. Mealog creates that ID on your device. It is not Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA) or any other Apple identifier, and it is not linked to your name, email or Apple Account. It is deleted when you delete the app or turn analytics off, and a new one is created only if you turn analytics back on.

Events never contain photos, voice, transcripts, text you type, meal entries, calories or other nutrition values, your goals, your profile or body details, or anything from Apple Health. They contain no prices, transaction IDs or payment details.

Your choice. If you are in the EU/EEA or the UK, analytics is off until you allow it. Mealog asks once, after onboarding, with “Allow” and “No thanks” given equal weight, and sends nothing before you answer. Elsewhere, including Switzerland, analytics is on by default and you can turn it off. Either way the switch is in Settings > AI & data > Share anonymous usage. Turning it off stops all events immediately and deletes the analytics ID from your device.

Legal basis. In the EU/EEA and the UK: your consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a); for storing and reading the ID on your device, Art. 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive as implemented locally, in Germany § 25(1) TDDDG). You can withdraw it at any time in Settings. Withdrawal does not affect events already sent. In Switzerland: our overriding legitimate interest in understanding and improving the app (FADP Art. 31(1)). You can object at any time by turning off Settings > AI & data > Share anonymous usage.

Who receives it. Events go to PostHog, a US company, which stores them in its EU cloud in Frankfurt, Germany. PostHog acts as our processor under its data processing agreement. Because PostHog is a US company, US authorities may be able to demand access to the data under US law (the CLOUD Act) even though it is stored in the EU. For any access from the United States, the transfer rests on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses with the Swiss addendum in PostHog's data processing agreement (GDPR Art. 46; FADP Art. 16).

We have set PostHog to discard your IP address and to do no location lookup. Its servers still see the IP address briefly when an event arrives, as any server does, but it is not stored with the event.

How long. Events are deleted automatically after 12 months.

6. What Mealog never does

  • No third-party analytics, telemetry or crash-reporting SDKs. Mealog sends only the product events listed in section 5, never meal content or health values.
  • No advertising, ad identifiers or tracking across apps and sites.
  • No selling, renting or trading of your data — to anyone, ever.
  • No profiles built about you, and no automated decisions with legal effects.

7. Who else is involved

  • OpenAI (OpenAI Ireland Ltd., Dublin; processing also by OpenAI OpCo, LLC, in the United States) — transcribes voice recordings, and analyses photos, transcripts and text, to produce nutrition estimates. Transfers to the United States rest on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (with the Swiss addendum) under OpenAI's data processing agreement.
  • Cloudflare (Cloudflare, Inc., USA) — runs the pass-through backend that forwards AI requests to OpenAI and enforces the daily limit; requests are handled in a Cloudflare data centre that may be outside Switzerland and the EU. It processes technical connection data (such as IP address and time), and does not store the photo, the voice recording, the transcript, the text or estimate. We keep no request logs of our own (Worker logging is off); Cloudflare may process connection data for network operation and security under its data processing agreement. Transfers rest on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (with the Swiss addendum) in that agreement.
  • Vercel — a fallback host for the same pass-through backend, used only if Cloudflare is unreachable. Vercel, Inc. is a US company certified under the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (register read 21.10.2026, Active).
  • PostHog, a US company, stores the product events and the random analytics ID described in section 5 in its EU cloud (Frankfurt, Germany), as our processor. IP addresses are discarded. Transfers rest on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (with the Swiss addendum) in its data processing agreement.
  • Apple — distributes the app and handles any purchase; and, if you turn on iCloud sync, stores your private iCloud database. Apple's own privacy policy governs the App Store, your purchases and iCloud.

Apart from the providers listed here, who process data only on our behalf or, for Apple, under its own terms, no one receives your data. We never sell or rent it.

8. How long anything is kept

On our side we keep no photos, voice recordings, transcripts, text or estimates. OpenAI may hold the inputs for up to 30 days as described above. On your side, entries stay until you delete them or remove the app — and, if iCloud sync is on, until you delete them from your synced devices. The daily-limit counter on our backend is deleted within 2 days after your last AI request. Product events (section 5) are deleted automatically after 12 months. The analytics ID on your device is deleted when you turn analytics off or delete the app.

9. Your rights

Under the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection — and under the GDPR if you are in the EU or EEA — you may request access to your personal data, correction, deletion, restriction, objection or portability, and you may withdraw your consent to AI estimation at any time. You may complain to a supervisory authority: in Switzerland the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner, and in the EU/EEA your local data-protection authority.

In practice most of these you can exercise yourself in seconds, because the data is on your phone: edit an entry to correct it, delete an entry or the app to erase it, export your log to take it elsewhere, withdraw AI consent and clear or disable your profile and Apple Health access in Settings. Because we keep no copy of your content — and hold no name, account or content of yours, only the short-lived IP-address counter described in section 4 and the product events described in section 5 — we cannot look up or retrieve the short-lived copy OpenAI may hold; a request about that goes to OpenAI. The product events carry only a random ID that we cannot connect to you, so we cannot find “your” events on request. To stop them and remove the ID from your device, turn analytics off in Settings. Events already sent are deleted automatically when the retention period in section 8 ends. For anything else, email v.skromny@bluewin.ch and you will get an answer within 30 days.

10. Security

Everything leaving the app travels over encrypted connections (TLS). Your log is protected by your device's own security — a passcode or Face ID is the single most effective thing you can do to protect it. No system is perfect, and we do not claim otherwise.

11. Children

Mealog is not meant for people under 16, and you should not use it if you are under 16. If we learn that a child has used it, write to us and we will help delete their data.
